# Benchmark review

The six images were reviewed as a controlled prompt snapshot, not as a model
ranking. Scores use a 1–5 editorial scale and describe only these runs.

## Mid-century Modern

The baseline already responds to the resident evidence: walnut storage, amber
light, olive and rust textile, records, plants, and cane-and-chrome chairs. It
also stays close to familiar current MCM retail shorthand. The guided output
creates a more materially layered hierarchy of books, records, ceramics,
timber, textile, metal, and glass, while avoiding atomic motifs and icon piles.

The guided image is not simply “better.” It appears to add a stronger crown
detail at the ceiling and its storage density may overstate comfortable
clearance. The baseline also appears to alter the fixed ceiling light. Both
need room-truth review.

## Bauhaus

The baseline balances soft retained seating with metal shelving, adjustable
lights, books, plants, one red artwork, and a graphic textile. Its geometry and
framed art still approach the style’s most familiar shorthand.

The guided output makes the activity and workshop lens more visible: pale
plywood, black framing, task surfaces, book storage, textile, and adjustable
light organize the room. The single red accent remains secondary. Its main
failure is scale: the wall of shelving could read as permanent and compresses
the sofa zone, so renter-safe scope and fit still need proof.

## Japandi

The baseline is calm but relies heavily on a paper lantern, slatted pale wood,
sparse dark ceramics, and beige restraint. More importantly, it appears to
cover or replace the locked radiator. This is the strongest room-preservation
failure in the set.

The guided image keeps the radiator visible, preserves normal seating height,
and uses pale and dark timber, woven textile, a few books, ceramics, a plant,
diffuse light, and negative space. It avoids the most obvious market tokens but
can still read as generic Scandinavian calm. Its new window curtain needs an
operation and heat-safety check.

## What this sample supports

The Atlas-guided prompts produced more materially relevant differentiation and
fewer obvious clichés in this snapshot. They did not guarantee architectural
preservation, circulation, reversible scope, or historical depth. The baseline
also succeeded on several resident priorities. A repeat run could differ.

Every image remains a visual approximation. Structured measurements and a
deterministic fit check remain authoritative.
